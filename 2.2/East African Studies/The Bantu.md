## Interlacustrine Bantu/ Western Bantu
This group was also referred to as the **Lake region Bantu.**
They entered East Africa between L.Edward and Albert and settled in the areas North and West of L. Victoria between 1000 – 1300 AD.  T
They were basically agriculturalists.
Others moved West to Kenya and Eastern Uganda.

## Central Bantu
These moved into East Africa between L. Edward and L. Tanganyika and first settled in Central Tanzania between 1000 –1300 AD for example, the Sukuma and Nyamwezi.  Others later crossed and settled in Tanzania Plateau and Taita Hills around 1300 AD. Others moved north.

## Highland Bantu
These settled in the Kenya Highlands around 1600 –1800 AD.  These include the Kamba, Kikuyu, Embu, Chuka.  They probably moved north from the Taita hills. Some writers have suggested that about 1300 the Chuka came up from the Tana river and were the first to arrive in the Mt. Kenya area. The Embu followed them, about 1425. The last group, the Kikuyu seem to have arrived in the Muranga by the middle of the 16th Century and in Kiambu by the end of the 17th Century.  The migration of the Embu further was halted by the Masaai.  The migrations of the Meru and related groups probably began at the Coast.

## Southern Tanzania Bantu
This group entered through L. Malawi and L. Tanganyika.  They are also believed to have come from East Africa around 1000 – 1300.  This group includes the Bena, Yao, Hehe. 

## Coastal Bantus
According to the tradition between about 1591 and 1698, the Swahili sections of Kilidini, Changamwe and Tangana moved to Mombasa and formed Kalindini town. Where they had earlier migrated from Shungwaya because of attacks by the Galla and Somali. The Segeju and various sections of the Milikenda and other Swahili groups to join the Kilindini group and related peoples on the mainland behind Mombasa later joined them.

The Pokomo and Segeju traditions also suggest the theory of dispersal from Shunguwaya. The Pokomo say that they descended from Shunguwaya Bantu immigrants. However, due to contacts with other groups, they became a mixed community with Galla, Swahili, Segeju and Somali blood.  The Segeju believed that in the second half of the 7th Century the Galla attacked their ancestors. One group fled to the Lamu islands and nearby areas where they intermarried with the local Swahili to form the present Bajun or Tikuu. A second group fled to the lower Tana  and ancestors of present- day Buu section of the Segeju left the lower Tana and moved to the present homes.

## Reasons for migration
1. Drought and famine due to overcrowding and unpredictable climate.
2. Population pressure which resulted in overcrowding, scarcity of grazing and agricultural land.
3. External attacks especially from the migration of the Arabs into West Africa.
4. Internal conflicts from other misunderstanding between different clans concerning ownership of agricultural land, grazing lands, watering areas.
5. Epidemics and diseases/ natural calamities e.g. flooding of rivers like Niger, Nagana caused by Tsetse files.
6. Search for fertile land since Bantus were predominately farmers.
7. Love for adventure, they wanted to find out what was happening in other areas.
8. Group influence, they had seen there relatives and friends move.
9. They were in search of water and pasture for their animals.
10. The iron-working culture had made them make the clearing of land for cultivation much faster and efficient.

## Positive Results
2. Introduction of iron working to east African tribes which relied on stone tools.
3. Introduction of new crops e.g. yams & bananas which in turn led to the increased knowledge of food.
4. They introduced a centralized administration, whereby a the king acted as the overall ruler under whom were other chiefs down to the lay person.
5. They introduced a system of building permanent homes.
6. They introduced subsistence agriculture whereby they grew enough for home consumption and the rest was kept in storage or exchanged in barter trade.
7. Transmission of languages.
8. They lead to a rise of large states and bigger tribes in E.A i.e the Baganda, Kikuyu and Nyamwezi.
9. The absorption other tribes e.g. hunter and gatherer tribes.
## Negative Effects
1. It led to the depopulation due to clashes and wars i.e The Zimba people were cannibalistic people that attacked people in the coast.
2. There was loss of culture due to absorption by the Bantu.
3. There was transformation of languages into new ones which led to the dying of other languages.
