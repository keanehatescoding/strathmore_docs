Examples
Apple Inc.:Apple, one of the world's most valuable tech companies, is a perfect example of a network organizational structure. Although the company is known for its innovative products, a significant portion of its operations is outsourced. 
Nike Inc.:The renowned global sports brand Nike operates a network structure that has fueled its growth. Nike focuses on design, development, and marketing while the production process is outsourced to independent manufacturers primarily located in low-cost countries.

Advantages 
1. A network structure provides unparalleled flexibility in a rapidly changing market landscape.
2. Network structures are typically less bureaucratic than their traditional hierarchical counterparts these leads to speedy decision making.
3. The fundamental principle of a network organizational structure is the separation of core competencies from other operational processes. This allows organizations to focus intensely on their key strengths and areas of expertise. 
Disadvantages
1. In a network organizational structure, organizations become heavily reliant on their partners for delivering certain operational aspects. This could lead to disruption of the organization's entire operations when due to delays or failure to deliver of the agreed standard.
2. Ensuring consistent quality across different operations and partners can be challenging. Since each partner operates autonomously, they might have their own standards and procedures which could vary from those of the organization. 
3. Given that outsourced partners manage their own operations independently, organizations have limited direct control over these functions. This could lead to lack of direct control and possibly challenges.
