## 1. Deontology
Deontology comes from the Greek word __Deon__ which means __duty__.
Deontology states the morality of a human action is purely dependent on whether it fulfills a duty or fails to do so.
It determines right and wrong based on a set of clearly defined rules.
A core principle of deontology is everyone has a __inherent worth__ and __dignity__ and should be treated with fairness and dignity
Another ckore principle is __universability__ which states that moral principles are universally applicable and impartial.
It also states some actions are always right or wrong regardless of the circumstances.
Short-comes: It is too rigid especially when a certain crime may have been necessary.

## 2. Consequentialism/Teleology

Teleology comes from the word __telos__ which means __ends__. 
It states that the morality of an action is concluded by it's consequences.
The ends justifies the means.
Advantages: It is more adaptive and uses context to consider the repercussions.
Short-come: It's could possibly lead to unethical behavior if the end of a result it emphasized over the means to reach the ends.

### A. Utilitarianism

It suggests the moral value of an action is measured by it's capability to maximize overall happiness and minimize overall suffering or utility. 
It focuses on the results or consequences of our actions and treats intentions as irrelevant.
There are typo types of utilitarianism:
1. __Act Utilitarianism__ - IN ANY SITUATION, you should choose the action that produces the greatest good for the greatest number.
2. __Rule Utilitarianism__ - we ought to live by rules that IN GENERAL are likely to lead to the greatest good for the greatest number.
Act always so as to produce the greatest good for the greatest number. - this is the principle of utility
Utilitarianism holds the purpose of engineers is to create technology that benefit the greatest number of people.
Short-come: It can lead to what can be termed as immoral behavior in the conquest to produce the greatest good for the most people.

### B.Rights
People have fundamental rights that need to be respected as long as they don't detriment other people rights.
This is especially important when upholding data privacy laws and building artificial intelligence systems.

### C. Fairness and Justice
People should be treated equally.
Those that do not have the same starting ground should be given more opportunities in order to level the playing ground.
Technology should be inclusive: accessible to everyone i.e it should not discriminate on gender, place, income etc.

### D. Common Good
Founded on the concept that society comprises of individuals whose welfare intricately linked with the community. I.e we are all connected.
We should work collaboratively to achieve shared goals and not just personal gain.
As computer engineers we should think about focus on building tech that solves problems that affect the entire society rather a select few people.

### E. Virtue
It involves cultivating positive traits, values within ourselves i.e honesty, integrity etc
This leads to building a strong moral character which helps to build the society.
This are good for building trust and transparency in tech society.

### F. Ethical Problem Solving
This is a step by step guide when navigating ethical dilemmas and involves the following steps:
Steps
1. Recognizing the issue
2. Gathering information
3. Evaluating information
4. Making a decision
5. Implementing the decision
6. Reflecting on the outcome
As computer scientists when engineering algorithms we must account for biases in them and take adequate measures to mitigate them.
When creating software we must weigh the potential benefits versus the potential harm.
