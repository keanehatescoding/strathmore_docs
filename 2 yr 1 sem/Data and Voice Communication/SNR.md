<abbr title="Signal to Noise Ratio">SNR</abbr> is a ratio of the signal power to noise power.
$$\text {SNR}=\frac{S}{N}=\frac {P_S}{P_N}$$
where $P_S$ is __signal power__ and $P_N$ is __noise power__.
In __decibels$(dB)$__
$$SNR_{dB}=10 \log(\frac{P_S}{P_N})$$
Since power$(P)$ is given by $\frac{V^{2}}{R}$ then it can be further expressed in terms of power as:
$$SNR=\frac{P_{S}}{P_N}=\frac{V_{S}^{2}}{R}\times \frac{1}{\frac{V_{N}^2}{R}}=\frac{V_{S}^{2}}{R}\times \frac{R}{V_{N}^2}=\frac{V_{S}^2}{V_{N}^2}={(\frac{V_S}{V_N})}^2$$
Therefore in terms of decibels it will be
$$SNR_{db}=10\log(\frac{V_S}{V_N})^2=20\log\frac{V_S}{V_N}$$
