## The Highland Nilotes
They are majorly comprised of the Kalenjin, Pokot and Elgeyo.
They have no taboo about fishing, hunting or food gathering.
Their original point of entry in Kenya is thought to have been somewhere in the region of Mount Elgon, on the western border with Uganda. From there, the Pokot moved north, whilst the various proto-Kalenjin tribes spread out east and south.

## The Lake Nilotes
They comprise of the Luo.
They were originally cattle herders, but switched to agricultural and fishing lifestyle.
Their arrival led to the displacement of many Bantu groups notably the Gusii, Kuria and Luhya who were forced to the Highlands East and North of the Lake.

## The plain Nilotes
They have total reliance on pastrolism, this is what differentiates them from the Lake Nilotes and the Highland Nilotes, as well as their taboos against agriculture, hunting and the eating of wildlife or fish.
This is indicative in their deeply-ingrained conservatism, which has helped them survive the sweeping changes of the twentieth almost intact: as a result, they are now among the most famous tribes in Africa: Maasai, Samburu and Turkana.
They comprise of the Maasai, Samburu and Turkana, El Molo, Iteso, Njemps(Il Chamus) and the Okiek.
Most of this communities speak ol-Maa the root of the Maasai and Samburu.
