Is ensuring your __intended performance__ actually measures up to your __actual performance__ and using corrective measures to reach the difference.
## Controlling methods 
1. Feed forward
2. Steering
3. Feedback/past action
## 1. Feed Forward controls
Are controls you put before the activity takes place. e.g. budgets, forecasting, steering committee, establishment of checkpoints, organizing meetings on daily/weekly basis, feasibility studies, establishment of work procedures, establishment of organizational structures, inspection of raw material, appointment of managers and supervisors.
## 2. Screening
This functions are monitor activities while in progress: they are intended to detect deviation in a timely manner so that necessary actions are taken. They are commonly used in manufacturing systems which implement automated control.
This can be done through periodic review of performance through performance appraisal.

## 3. Feedback/Post Action controls
Implemented after an activity has taken place examples; end of semester examinations, end of year/annual reports, external auditors reports, inspection of finished goods before dispatch.
(i) The controlling process
1. Establish goals and standard
2. Measuring your actual performance against your standard
3. Comparing actual performance with the set standard and goals
4. Taking corrective actions e.g. delegate more staff, allocate more resources, motivating worker.
## Importance of Controlling
1. Contributes to achievement of set standards.
2. It directs the behaviour of employees towards goal achievements.
3. Improving motivation of employees.
4. Improves the coordination of activities in the organization.
## Limitations
1. When excessively implemented it can lead to becoming a very time consuming process.
2. It can lead to customer dissatisfaction when it becomes too time consuming.
3. Limits the level of creativity of workers.
4. Demoralizes workers.
5. Expensive to implement.
## Effective control measures
1. Focus on critical points
2. Integration into established process
3. Acceptance by employees
4. Availability of information when needed.
5. Economic feasibility
6. Accurate
7. Comprehensible
