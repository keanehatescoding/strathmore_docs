## Explanation

This is a organizational structure where each multiple members of different teams report to both a project manager and a department head.
A matrix organizational structure combines elements of both the divisional structure and the functional structure.

|           | Dep 1 | Dep 2  | Dep 3   |
| --------- | ----- | ------ | ------- |
| Project A | Steve | Angel  | Collins |
| Project B | Mary  | Claire | Colleen |
| Project C | Mark  | Ronald | Andrea  |

## Types of Matrix Organisation Structures

1. Weak/Functional matrix - the project managers have limited power while the functional managers is primarily responsible.
2. Balanced/Functional matrix - the project managers have are assigned to oversee the project and power is assigned equally between the functional head and the project manager.
3. Strong/Project matrix - the project managers is primary responsible 
## Examples

An IT company that has various departments like research, development, security and finance branch.
A drinks company that has various teams and department like testing, research, manufacture, finance and sales.

## Advantages

1. It creates open communication between different team members which allows innovative solutions
2. It creates free flow of information since each team reports to different project managers which in turn could lead to increased productivity and collaboration.
3. It allows the efficient use of resources by inclusion of specialists from other teams to a separate team which in turn leads overhead costs.
## Disadvantages

1. It may lead to a complex reporting style since team members may have trouble knowing who to and what to report to.
2. It could lead to slow response time which leads to further delays due to requirement of reporting to multiple people.
3. The task of juggling priorities between the project managers and department heads could be hard especially when both managers consider their work important.
