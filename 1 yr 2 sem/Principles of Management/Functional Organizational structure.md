A functional structure is defined as a system where employees are grouped in different departments based on expertise, each having a functional head. Unlike a divisional organizational structure, employees are organized by function rather than by authority.

| Dep 1(Accouting) | Dep 2(Marketing) | Dep 3(IT)      |
| ---------------- | ---------------- | -------------- |
| Accountant       | Promoter         | IT consolutant |
| Auditor          | Influencer       | Developer      |
| CFA              | Editor           | Database Admin |
## Advantages

1. Clear decision-making process - because each employee isn’t individually managed, functional organizational designs allow for more flexibility and autonomy within each department.
2. Grouping based on expertise - by strictly working within their field, employees can focus entirely on their own domain of expertise.
3. Easier measurement and development - because employees are grouped by fields of expertise, it becomes easier to evaluate their performance.
4. Since employees are grouped based on their expertise it can increase productivity as their are entirely familiar with their department.
5. It allows for standardisation which can lead to consistency and streamlined operations.

## Disadvantages 

1. It could lead isolation since employees are grouped by expertise, cross-functional collaboration is not encouraged
2.  It can lead to slow decision making as any collaboration needs approval from several functional managers.
3. Functional structures can prevent this creativity from unfolding, as the model discourages employees from cross-functional collaboration.
4. If cross-collaboration is absolutely needed it can make it very hard to accomplish.

## Examples 

A large company that operates with a structure that has functional elements is Unilever, the owner of brands such as Knorr, Dove, and Ben & Jerry’s. In late 2022, Unilever announced changes to its organizational structure to make it “simpler, more category-focused”.

It moved away from its [[Matrix Structure|matrix structure]] and organized itself into five departments focused on Beauty & Well-Being, Personal Care, Nutrition, Home Care, and Ice Cream. Each department, or Business Group as Unilever calls it, is responsible for its own operation.

This structure has elements of a divisional organizational structure, but also of a functional structure, since the departments each have a specialized focus and operate in isolation from each other.
