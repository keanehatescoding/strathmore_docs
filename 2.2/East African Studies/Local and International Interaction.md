## Indian and Arab Influence
The Indian Ocean coast was populated by the Bantu-Speaking farmers, cattle keepers and fishers. These people formed small village settlement on estuaries and islands and built small boats for fishing, communicating, and trading along the coast. This region was known to Greek and Roman traders of the early centuries AD as **Azania**.
By this time Bantu settlements stretched from the Kenyan island of Lamu in the north to the Rufiji estuary near modern-day Dar es Salaam, Tanzania, in the south.
These communities exported ivory, rhinoceros horn, tortoise shell, and other goods.
With the on-going spread of Islam, from the 8th century the communities of the East African coast became more directly connected to the long-distance trading network of the Indian Ocean. Muslim Arabic-speaking traders settled along the coast and married into local ruling families. The language and culture that developed remained distinctly African, but with Arabic and Islamic borrowings and influences. This language and culture, and the people in general, are referred to as **Swahili.** 
## Portuguese at the East African Coast
### Reasons
1. They needed to establish a commercial empire in order to get the product of East Africa i.e ivory, gold and silk.
2. They wanted to maintain control of the main trading towns.
3. They wanted to defeat the muslim traders and rulers who had monopolized the Indian Ocean Trade.
4. They wanted to prevent other European powers from getting access to the Indian Ocean Trade.
5. Desire to get revenue for the development of their country.
6. The coast had good deep natural harbours where ships could anchor on their way to and from the East.
7. The coast was strategically place and this would enable them to control sea pirates and  other rival powers.
8. They wanted to revenge the muslim arabs who had conquered portugal in 711 AD by converting them to Christianity and stop the spread of Islma.
9. They hoped to get help from King Prester John thought to be in the interior of the North East Africa. They hoped the king would help them in a crusade against the muslims on the coast.
10. They had hopes of stopping Egyptians and Turks from sending military aid to their muslim brothers in the coast.
11. They were interested in exploration and adventure ad this was the period of Renaissance( born again/ change) in Europe. 


### Reasons why the Portuguesse wee successful
1. They had superior weapons.
2. They had well trained soldiers with superior skills of fighting compared to the coastal people.
3. They had better and faster ships(carracks) which were well equipped for naval warefare.
4. The coastal people were disunited which gave a chance for the Portuguese to fight isolated enemies i.e Sofala briefly submerged to Portuguese domain as opposed to Kilwa rule which they lamented, Malindi refused to unite with Mombasa.
5. Some coastal towns like Kilwa were caught unaware.
6. The constant attack on coastal towns by the Galla, Zimba and Turkish had weakened their defences.
7. The Portuguesse were financially equipped to go to wars with the coastal towns.
8. The coastal towns had very weak economies which could not sustain prolonged war with the economically strong Portuguese.

### Reasons for the decline of the Portuguese Rule
1. Portugal was a small country that could not provide enough administrators and officials for such a large coastline
2. It had few soldiers and could not keep fortified garrison along the coast.
3. Authority was left in the hands of incompetent and corrupt officials who were enriching themselves
4. The Africans hated the Portuguese due to differences in religion.
5. The Portuguese failed to support their own allies at the coast, some even betrayed them.
6. Portugal had been forced into a union with Spain between 1540-1640, which weakened her control of the trading colonies as she was no longer interested in the overseas empire.
7. The Portuguese were challenged by other European powers which began competing with the Portuguese for the Indian Ocean.
8. The coastal people found useful allies against the Portuguese i.e the Oman Arabs, Turks.
9. They were faced with constant rebellion along the coast i.e Pate & Mombasa.
10. Tropical diseases which claimed a lot of the Portuguese's life i.e. Malaria.
11. The Portuguese were greatly weakened by a group of cannibals, the Zimba.
12. The unhealthy climate made life difficult for them to work i.e some parts were too hot and humid while others were too cold.
13. The distance between the East African Coast and Portugal was too far hence reinforcements were delayed.
14. The income obtained from the Sofala gold trade was not enough to pay for administration coasts
15. The Portuguese refused to learn local languages hence their was a problem of language barrier.
16. The Portuguese lost Muz in 1622 to the Persians during the struggle with Arabs and  in 1650 the Oman Arabs won back Muscat.
17. The Capture of Fort Jesus their stronghold in 1698 by the Omani greatly contributed to their decline.

### Positive Effects of the Portuguese Rule
1. They built Fort Jesus which at the coast which became a fortress and later a tourist attraction for centuries.
2. They enriched the Swahili Language with the addition of new 60 words i.e emeza -> table and pesa -> money.
3. They introduced new crops from South Africa i.e. cassava, pawpaws, oranges, sweet potatoes, guavas, pineapples, mangoes, maize.
4. They made an improvement to ship construction.
5. The introduction of new methods of farming i.e use of cow dung as manure. 
6. They broke the Muslim-Arab monopoly in the Indian Ocean.
7. They led to the coming of more European and Asian traders and craftsmen especially those who helped in the building of Fort Jesus. 

### Negative
1. There was heavy loss of life during the attacks.
2. Trade decline due to constant wars and rebellion.
3. The was decline in the coastal due to many being burnt down and left in ruins i.e Kilwa and Mombasa
4. There was widespread poverty and misery among the coastal people due to decline in trade.
5. Their was destruction of property and crops which led to famine and starvation.
6. The coastal people suffered oppression and brutality during the Portuguese Rule.
7. Their was depopulation due to many wars  in the areas smuggling developed because the Portuguese had failed to establish proper trading links with the Interior.
8. Some towns were banned from trading with their initial partners which led to their decay i.e Gedi.
9. They led to the European interest at the coast hence leading to the colonization in the 19th Century.
