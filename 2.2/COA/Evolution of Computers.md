## Analog Computers
### 1. Tally Sticks
A tally stick was an ancient memory aid device to
record and document numbers, quantities, or even
messages.

### 2. Abacus
Invented in Babylonia in 2400 BC.

### 3. Napier Bones
Invented by __John Napier__ in 1614.
Allowed the operator to multiply, divide and calculate square and cube roots by moving the rods around and placing them in specially constructed boards.
![[Pasted image 20251104180252.png]]
### 4. Slide Rule
Invented by __William Oughtred__ in 1622.
It was based on Napier's ideas about logarithms.
Used primarily for
1. Multiplication
2. Division
3. Roots
4. Logarithms
5. Trigonometry
![[Pasted image 20251104180316.png]]

### 5. Pascaline
Invented by Blaise Pascal in 1642
Limited only to addition and subtration.
It was too expensive to acquire.
![[Pasted image 20251104180531.png]]
### 6. Step Reckoner
Invented by Gottfried Wilhelm Leibniz in 1672.
It was used for addition, subtraction, multiplication and division operations
![[Pasted image 20251104181059.png]]
### 7. Jacquard Loom
The Jacquard loom is a mechanical loom, invented by Joseph-Marie Jacquard in 1803.
It an automatic loom controlled by punched cards.

![[Pasted image 20251104181040.png]]
### 8. Arithmometer
The machine could perform the four basic mathematic functions addition, subtraction, division and multiplication.
![[Pasted image 20251104181139.png]]

### 9. The Difference and Analytical Engine
It an automatic, mechanical calculator designed to tabulate polynomial functions.
Difference engine only did addition operation while analytical did all the four operations
Invented by Charles Babbage in 1822 and 1834 respectively.
It is the first mechanical modern computer.
![[Pasted image 20251104181401.png]]
### 10. Scheutzian Calculation Engine
Invented by Per Georg Scheutz in 1843.
Based on Charles Babbage's difference engine
It was the first __printing calculator__.
![[Pasted image 20251104181548.png]]
### Tabulating Machine

## Electronic Computers

### 2. Z1 
The __first programmable computer.__
• Created by Konrad Zuse in Germany from 1936 to 1938.
• To program the Z1 required that the user insert punch tape into a punch tape reader and all output
was also generated through punch tape

### 3. Atanasoff-Berry Computer (ABC)

There are five generations of computer namely:
• First generation – 1946 - 1958
• Second generation – 1959 - 1964
• Third generation – 1965 - 1970
• Fourth generation – 1971 - today
• Fifth generation – Today to the future
### First Generation Computers aka (Thermionic Valves/ Vacuum tubes)
#### Examples
ENIAC, UNIVAC I
#### Disadvantages
1. Expensive to purchase
2. Expensive to replace
3. Constantly overheated and therefore needed frequent replacement
4. Were energy inefficient
5. Bulky as in took a lot of space and therefore making computers the size of rooms
### Second Generation (Transistors)
#### Examples
IBM 7094, UNIVAC 1107
#### Advantages
1. Cheaper to purchaes
2. More energy efficient
3. Faster
4. Took little space
5. Did not overheat
6. Cheaper to replace malfunctioning transitors

### Forth Generation (Microprocessors)
The microprocessor brought the fourth generation of computers, as thousands of integrated circuits
were built onto a single silicon chip.
As these small computers became more powerful, they could be linked together to form networks, which
eventually led to the development of the Internet.
Fourth generation computers also saw the development of GUIs,the mouse and handheld devices.
#### Examples
IBM PC, Apple Macintosh
