## Methods used to establish colonial rule
## British Indirect Rule 
### Reasons why the British used Indirect Rule
1. Traditional rulers were accepted by the masses.
2. It was cheaper than direct rule since the rulers were paid in the form of gifts i.e old clothes, simple salaries and presents.
3. They had a belief that African societies were static and their political institutions were fixed.
4. There was a shortage of white personnel.
5. The British feared immediate African response, so African chiefs would act as shock absorbers to which their manpower capacity would not easily shoulder.
6. The traditional leaders understood the people better and they spoke the same or related language.
7. Some supporters of this system argued that it imparted skills of leadership to African leaders.
8. It had worked in Nigeria, India and Canada.
9. Some areas were physical hard to reach for the British to administer as opposed to indirect rule.
10. It was also a way of solving the language barrier problem between the British and the African communities.
11. Failure of Assimilation: this proved to be impossible because Africans remained Africans (insisted on their cultures). Africans greatly failed to turn into black Europeans.

### How indirect rule was practised.
Indirect rule was most successful in centralised states like Buganda.  Here, it was applied at the level of local governments but not at the level of central governments because such offices were reserved for the whites.

The African chief was the main actor at local levels like districts and counties. Policies were got from the central government and were passed to lesser chiefs who in turn passed over the policies on the local man.

The lesser chiefs operated at the lowest levels for example the sub-county chiefs, village headmen, parish chiefs and clan elders.  Therefore they provided basis of political authority.

Grades of chiefs used in various parts of Uganda included, County chiefs, Sub-county Chiefs, Parish chiefs.
Functions of African Chiefs
1. Collection of taxes
2. Implementation of government policy
3. Promotion of law and order
4. Presided over local native law courts

### Problems faced by the British during indirect rule
1. In decentralized communities they found it difficult to accept a single accepted authority at the top
2. The frequent wars of resistance especially in Bunyoro hindered the implementation of the indirect rule.
3. There was lack of trained manpower especially since then there had been only 40 trained personnel for the Ugandan colonial administration.
4. The system faced the problem of administration costs especially since the colonial government never wanted British tax payer money to finance the administration of Uganda.
5. At times the British failed to understand the social organization of the people.
6. The policy implementers also faced the problem of foreign geography and diseases especially in the interior.  This worked against indirect rule.
7. Where African chiefs were strong, they did not want to co-operate with the British at all. This was true with Chief Awich of Payera in northern Uganda.
8. Some chiefs never wanted to hand over taxes collected to the British treasury and it caused financial problems.
9. Most chiefs embarked on fulfilling their personal needs i.e Kakungulu in Bukedi was aiming at creating his own political kingdom before he was demoted.
10. Most Africans chiefs lacked the formal education and skills necessary for the job.
11. Their was the problem of language barrier where the British supervisors were unable to communicate with the local rulers.
12. The chiefs appointed by the British were unpopular.

### Effects of Indirect Rule
1. Indirect rule encouraged parochialism among the natives and their rulers which made it difficult to eradicate even during the post-independence period.
2. Indirect rule also greatly retarded the progress of self governance for Africans by excluding the elite society from participating in government. It is little wonder therefore that the policy greatly registered resistances from the elite class in areas such as Nigeria, Ghana among others where it started from.
3. It also imposed British autocracy on the people under the pretence of training the African traditional rulers for self-governance.
