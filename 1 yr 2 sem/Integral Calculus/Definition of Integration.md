Integration is asking which function when differentiated gives you the resulting expression.
To explain this better lets say we have a function f(x) which is the derivative of another function say F(x) i.e
$$ F'(x) = f(x)$$
asking ourselves what function is being differentiated to get f(x) we get the following expression which is slightly wrong.
$$F(x) = \int f(x)dx$$
To make it right we ought to add a +c is because when we differentiate any function with a constant say $x^5+ 5$ the derivative of the constant term is zero since the derivative of a constant term is zero. Since we cannot determine the original constant after integrating then we add a +c to represent this constant.
Therefore a formal mathematical definition of integration is 
$$ \int f(x)dx = F(x) +c $$
where $\int$ is the integral symbol
	$f(x)$ is the integrand (function being integrated)
	 $dx$ is the what we are integrating with respect to aka the differential
	 $F(x)$ the original function before differentiation
	 $c$ the constant term in the original function.
_NB:_ The $dx$ is very important because it is telling us what expressions are we integrating, think of $\int$ as the opening bracket and $dx$ the closing bracket. It will also be important when we are learning $u$ substitution so never omit it.
