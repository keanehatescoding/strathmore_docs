**Nationalism** refers to the rise of consciousness and love for one's country.
## Characteristics of Nationalism in East Africa
1. Rise of political parties i.e Uganda National Congress (UNC), KANU, KADU, TANU.
2. It was led by a few educated Africans i.e Julius Nyerere, Milton Obote and Jomo Kenyatta.
3. Most nationalistic activities were centered in towns like Nairobi, Mombasa and Dar-es-Salaam.
4. Violent groups were formed to engage with colonialists by force .i.e Mau Mau in Kenya.
5. There was constant arrest and detention of nationalistic leaders .i.e  Kenyatta was imprisoned in 1953, Obote forced out of Makerere College, Nyerere and many others too were detained several times.
6. The cold war conflict remained clear among the East African nationalists. Nyerere for instance adopted the socialist ideology through his popular Ujamaa policy.
7. The press became a key tool of nationalism in East Africa. News papers like MUNNO, Ngabo ( Uganda), radio stations, magazines were used in the spread of nationalist ideas.
8. Negotiations also characterized nationalism in East Africa i.e constitutional reforms.
9. Trade unions were also formed to call for worker rights i.e Young Kikuyu Association in 1921.

## Factors for the Growth of Nationalism in East Africa.
1. Evils of colonialism i.e forced labour, over taxation, land alienation, racial discrimination and forced growing of cash crops made Africans hate their colonial masters.
2. Western education led to the rise and growth of African Nationalism.
3. WW2 also encouraged nationalism as Africans learnt a lot from WW2 i.e democracy, white supremacy was a myth etc.
4. The formation of the United Nations in 1945 which began to pressure countries to grant independence to their colonies.
5. The Atlantic Charter of 1941 which president F.Roosevelt (U.S.A) called for the respect of people's right to choose a government of their will.
6. The 1945 Manchester Pan African conference helped African nationalism. Delegates including Nkrumah etc called for states to use all means to fight against colonialism.
7. The formation of political parties also encouraged nationalism in East Africa. Parties like KANU, KADU (KENYA) TANU (TZ) UNC UPC, DP, and KY (Uganda) mobilized the people in demanding for independence.
8. The Mau Mau uprising of 1952-1956 set an example for countries such as Uganda and Tanzania.
9. The rise of able and charismatic leaders such as Jomo Kenyatta , Julius Nyerere, Milton Obote, Abu Mayanja and others. These led the masses in popular demonstrations and rallies demanding for independence

## Rule played by political parties in the Struggle for Uganda's Independence
1. Parties trained leaders who helped mobilize the masses for independence.
2. They sensitized and educated Ugandans on the political state of the country.
3. They called for the independence of Uganda raising massive support to the independence movement.
4. They organized peaceful demonstrations against policies like taxation, cash crop growing.
5. Parties sent representatives in the pre-independence negotiations and last-minute constitutional preparations.
6. They mobilized funds to finance political activities.
7. Recruited the youths into active party service thus training a generation that was to lead Uganda to independence such as Mayanja Nkangi , Ignatius Musaazi .etc.
8. Violent action organized by the various parties pressurised the colonialists to grant independence. Boycotts, attacks on foreigners and torching (burning) houses all speeded up the process to decolonize Uganda.
## Factors which lead to the Attainment of independence in Uganda
1. The second world war, Ex – soldiers came back with military skills and militant ideas leading to violent action like burning of white owned houses.
2. Rise of the labour party in British in 1945 which led to the appointment of some Ugandans in Legco.
3. The Manchester Conference of 1945 which helped nationalism in Uganda by use of any means i.e force to gain independence.
4. Western education had aided in training leaders for the independence movement.
5. The roads, railways and other infrastructure made by the colonial government were used by nationalists i.e Obote to traverse the whole of Uganda for support.
6. Formation of political parties i.e UNC and later UNP, DP.
7. The continued exploitation by Asians and whites of Uganda’s resources like copper in Kilembe provoked anger among Ugandans.
8. The development of urban centers e.g. Jinja, Kampala favored nationalism.
9. Asian countries like India which had already got independence (1947) helped Uganda too. Some Ugandan nationalist like Abu Mayanja, Bidandi Sali e.tc. Studied in India or attended conferences there.
10. The 1952 revolution in Egypt also influenced nationalism in Uganda Nasser , the new president of Egypt assisted Ugandans like Ignatius Musaazi of UNC (Uganda National Congress)
11. The United Nations organization also put pressure on British to decolonize even Uganda.
12. The British policy of favoring Buganda more than the rest of Uganda. This made the rest of Ugandan’s unite against the Baganda first and finally the colonialists too.
13. Ghana’s early independence in 1957 also inspired strong demand for independence in 1957 also inspired strong demand for independence in Uganda too.
## Major obstacles in Uganda's Struggle for Independence
1. High levels of illiteracy in Uganda.
2. Lack of a common language in Uganda.
3. Trade Union activities were limited in Uganda.
4. The limitation of the press, i.e The few news papers such as “Uganda Eyogera” were in Luganda hence only read by Baganda. This isolated the other Ugandan’s from ideas in the paper.
5. Some of the elites were puppets of the British colonial government and hence could not join political paties like UNC, UPC etc.
6. The delay to establish contacts between nationalists in Uganda and outside Africa also delayed the struggle. Very few Ugandans had traveled to democratic states like USA to borrow the spirit of freedom and hence few would challenge colonialism as an abuse of human rights.
7. The formation of political parties too delayed independence. Active party politics did not come until after 1950.
8. Religious divisions between the catholics and the protestants.
9. The common hated towards the Baganda.
10. Secessionist attempts by Buganda.
11. Ideological differences between the different political parties i.e DP leaders had capitalistic sentiments while UNC and UPC were pro-socialits.

# Nationalism in Tanganyika
## Reasons for Tanganyika's early independence
1. Formation of political clubs/association e.g. TAA and TANU.
2. The spirit of unity that came as a result of the existence of an all embracing Political party- TANU.
3. The personality of the colonial governors favored Tanzania’s early independence. Governor Richard Turnbull for example encouraged preparations for Tanzania’s Independence.
4. The United Nations, supervised preparations for independence in Tanganyika through its Trusteeship Council.
5. Racial or tribal differences were greatly limited in Tanganyika unlike Kenya and Uganda.
6. Political activities were not much restricted by the authority in Tanganyika as was in Kenya and Uganda. Party activities for instance would be left free.
7. Tanganyika’s adoption of Kiswahili as a common language promoted unity in the call for independence.
8. The able leadership of men like Julius Nyerere who mobilised all Tanganyika’s without discriminating based on race.
