## Stages of Moral development
1. Pre-conventional
2. Conventional
3. Post-conventional /Autonomous

### 1. Pre-Conventional Stage
The participants are believed to not be able to be accountable to their actions.
Involves punishment or reward for an action.
Occurs between pre-school and early puberty.

### 2. Conventional Stage
At this stage to try and conform to your peers and try to stay loyal to authority without much questioning.
It is also called herd mentality, __heteronymous thinking__.
Also characterized by common sense thinking.
#### Characteristics of Common Sense
1. Habitual and imitative
2. Vague/Ambigiuous
3. Unreflective and untested belief
4. Seldom accompanies explanation and when present are weak.

### 3. Post-Conventional Stage
At this stage you start to question accepted norms.
Morality is characterized by principles and morals that you have thought and criticized yourself.
Happens during adulthood and 

__Moral theory__ consists of a set of moral principles.
A moral theory can have 5 different purposes namely:
1. Keep society from falling apart.
2. Reduce/Stop human suffering
3. Promote human flourishing
4. Resolve conflict of interest in just and orderly ways.
5. Assign praise & blame, reward & punishment and guilt.

### Features of Moral Principles
1. __Prescriptivity__
2. __Universalizability__
3. __Overridingness__
4. __Publicity__
5. __Practicality__

# Ethical Principles
## 1. Principle of Beneficence
It states that we owe a duty or obligation to help others pursue their interests or well-being.
However in doing so, one must not subject oneself to a significant risk.
The duty of beneficence is founded on:
1. __Fair play__ i.e affirmative action
2. __Reciprocity__ - return favors to others as we have benefited from them.

## 2. Principle of non-maleficence
This is the negative side of beneficence.
It states that we have a duty not to harm others.

## 3. Respect of Persons
It incorporates 3 basic ethical requirements:
1. Everyone has an intrinsic value and therefore has to be treated with respect and dignity.
2. Treat persons as autonomous agents.
3. Protect persons with diminished autonomy.

## 3. Principles of impartiality or nondiscrimination
Each person's interest and well-being are equally important from a the moral point of view.

# Frameworks for Ethics
1. __Teleology/Consequentialist theories__ - what action that brings the most desirable consequences ought I do?
2. __Deontological ethics__ - what I ought to do.
3. __Virtue ethics__ - what kind of person I ought to be.

## 1. Consequentialist Theories
### 1. Utilitarianism/ Ethical Universalism
Actions are right or wrong solely by virtue of their consequences and nothing else matters i.e actions are not right or wrong in themselves but their evaluated based on their utility.
#### Strengths of Utilitariansm
1. Modern utilitarianism has been very influential in social reforms and modern policy formulation.
2. It looks at the particular impact of an action and how it affects people.
3. Economists share the concept of utility.
4. Since it tends to seek the happiness of the majority this cause it to defeat egoistic tendencies.
5. It has stimulus for social improvement since it recognizes humans as social beings.
6. It promotes public good as by nature it tries to achieve common good.

#### Weaknesses
1. It may ignores other moral characteristics i.e justice, rights, keeping promises.
2. It may sanction the use of persons as mere means instead of requiring us to respect persons as ends.
3. There is also the problem of how to determine or calculate happiness or pleasure i.e. how to calculate standard pleasure.

## 2. Deontological Theories
### Kant's Theory
Morality is a matter of following absolute rules or duties.
Morality is also based on categorical imperatives namely:
1. Morality does not depend on a particular desire or situation
2. Moral obligation is categorical i.e you ought to do such and such and period.
3. Moral principles are based on the fact every rational person must accept them.

The  principles of Kant's Categorical Imperatives
1. __Act only according to the maxim by which you will at the same time will that it becomes a universal law__
2. __Act so as to treat humanity whether in your own person that of another as end never as mere means.__

#### Advantages of Kant's Theory
1. There exists a consistency in application of morality.
2. Promotes the golden rule i.e universality and reciprocity
3. No one is special in terms of morality.
4. Morality is independent of personal conveniences and thus immune to human prejudice.
5. A good judgement must be supported by good reasons.

#### Weakness of Kan't Theory
1. Ignores consequences in determining the rightness or wrongness of actions.
2. The idea that morality is absolute makes morality unable fail to accept extenuating circumstances.
3. Renders morality unable to solve conflicts in a situation of conflict of values.

## 3. Virtue Ethics
For this type of ethics the primary question is not what I ought to do but what kind of person oughtn't I be.
Ethics cannot be reduced to the mere fulfilment of rules or norms.

Limits of norms/rules:
1. Impossible to make for every situation.
2. There lacks a rule/law that prescribes the duty to take care of one's parent.
3. They do not necessarily inspire us to be good persons/burdensome.
