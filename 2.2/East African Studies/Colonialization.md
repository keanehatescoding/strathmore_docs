## Cause of the Scramble and Partition of Africa
1. Belief in an abundance of minerals in Africa.
2. The Europeans wanted to spread their western civilization and this was the western culture since Africa was previously referred to as ‘The Dark Continent’.
3. Since the Europeans had undergo an industrial revolution they were looking for raw materials for their industries.
4. They also wanted to obtain a market for their manufactured goods.
5. They wanted to spread Christianity.
6. They wanted to spend their surplus income in East Africa for high profits.
7. The Europeans were overpopulated and therefore they wanted new areas for settlement.
8. They also wanted to get colonies which were viewed as prestigious.
9. The industrial revolution led to unemployment which in turn allowed these unemployed people to try and find work in these colonies.
10. The rise of nationalism in Europe, European countries developed national pride in superiority over others. Therefore possession of colonies became a symbol of superiority. For example Germany and Italy were moved by that influence. 
11. Strategic reasons also led to the scramble and partition of East Africa.  Following the construction of the Suez Canal, Britain realised that Egypt could not survive on its own without the Nile; she therefore colonized Sudan and Uganda.
12. Some East African tribes were hostile to the early Europeans for example explorers, missionaries and traders. They therefore wanted to protect them in East Africa by taking full control. 
## Effects of the Scramble and Partition in East Africa.
1. East Africa was divided among the various European powers.
2. The East African people lost their independence.
3. Many chiefs and kings were killed or sent to exile.
4. They established new systems of administration i.e indirect rule by the British and Direct rule by the Germans.
5. Africans were considered inferior and were forced to provide labour for plantations and  public works.
6. There was an increase of Europeans in African lands i.e Kenyan Highlands.
7. Slave trade was completely wiped and replaced with legitimate trade.
8. It accelerated the construction and development of infrastructure such as schools and hospitals.
9. There was maximum exploitation of resources i.e gold, copper and cash crops.
10. Introduction of new methods of farming.
11. The promotion of western culture into African communities i.e western dressing.
12. Taxes were introduced which were paid in the form of money i.e hut & gun tax.
13. Western education was introduced which replaced African education.

## African Response to Colonialism
African societies responded in different ways to European occupation. Those African societies or leaders that right from the start of colonialism decided to fight Europeans are known as primary resistors. They include Kabarega of Bunyoro, Nandi of Kenya and Hehe under Mkwawa.
Those who welcomed Europeans and only decided to fight after realizing that Europeans had taken their independence and wealth are called secondary resistors. Examples include Buganda under Mwanga. 
Then there were communities who cooperated with Europeans. These are known as collaborators. They include Buganda, Ankole, Maasai among others. 
Another category included opportunists, mercenaries or career collaborators. These supported colonialists in exchange for material gains. Examples include Apollo Kagwa and Semei Kakungulu of Buganda. 
Some communities also did not mind whether they were colonized or not.

### Reasons for collaboration
1. They thought this would protect their independence as they never expected the colonial masters to betray them. They never understood the signing of treaties meant they lost their independence.
2. They wanted to get weapons and other military support against their enemies.
3. They collaborated because of military weakness i.e the decline of the Maasai led to Lenana signing treaties with the British to safeguard his rule.
4. Natural calamities had forced some communities to sign treaties i.e smallpox and rinderpest had weakened the Maasai.
5. Internal conflict, Laibon Lenana of the Maasai wanted protection against his brother Sendeyo.
6. To acquire wealth and the missionaries had told them they were primitive and backward.
7. Other Africans collaborated because they hoped to loot property after the defeat of African resistors. An example is Luo support to the British against the Nandi in present day Kenya.
8. Missionary influence i.e missionaries had convinced Africans to sign treaties.
9. Some communities collaborated because their enemies had been resisting European domination i.e the Baganda partly collaborated because the Banyoro resisted British rule.
10. Some African communities collaborated because they were weakened by slave trade.
## Why some Africans resisted Colonial rule
1. They wanted to preserve their independence.
2. They had a strong confidence in their warriors.
3. They could not trust the Europeans, because the Europeans supported their enemies i.e Kabarega resisted because the Baganda collaborated with the British.
4. Clash of interest, they wanted to expand their territory and this clashed with the European's interests.
5. They felt betrayed by the Europeans i.e Kabaka Mwanga.
6. They wanted to preserve their economic independence i.e Abushiri Rebellion because the Germans started imposing taxes on trade and began to grab people's property.
7. Unfair colonial practices i.e taxation, forced labour and interference with African traditional religion & culture.

### Role of Christian Missionaries in the Colonisation of East Africa
1. They supplied information to the colonialists which they utilized to plan how to effectively impose their colonial rule.
2. They had a reciprocal relationship with the colonialists whereby the laid the ground work for colonialists.
3. They enhanced the growth of tropical raw materials i.e cotton, coffee etc. i.e Bishop K. Boroup introduced cotton in Uganda.
4. The appealed to their home governments for protection.
5. Religion was a mechanism of divide and rule i.e converts and non-converts hated each other and the Europeans used this divide to their advantage.
6. Mission stations served as colonial government headquarters. The established mission infrastructure was used to help in the establishment and sustenance of European colonial rule.
7. Missionary stations served as military bases from where the European colonial forces launched attacks on the resisting Africans. African Lugard used old Kampala hill as a military base against Kabalega.
8. They acted as interpreters for Africans during signing of treaties.
