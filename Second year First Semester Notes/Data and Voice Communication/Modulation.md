Broadly speaking modulation is classified into two categories namely:
1. Analog modulation
2. Digital modulation

Analog modulation is then further subdivided into another 2 categories namely:
1. Continuous wave modulation
2. Pulse modulation

Under continuous wave modulation is then we divide it into 3 categories i.e.
1. __[[Amplitude Modulation]]__
2. __[[Frequency modulation]]__
3. __[[Phase modulation]]__

In pulse modulation we have 4 different types namely:
1. <abbr title="Pulse Amplitude Modulation">PCM</abbr>
2. <abbr title="Pulse Width Modulation">PWM</abbr>
3. <abbr title="Pulse Position Modulation">"PPM"</abbr>
4. <abbr title="Pulse Code Modulation">PCM</abbr>

For digital modulation we have 3 types i.e.
1. <abbr title="Amplitude Shift Keying">ASK</abrr>
2. <abbr title="Frequency Shift Keying">FSK</abbr>
3. <abbr title="Phase Shift Keying">PSK</abbr>

There are 6 types of modulation but we will focus on the first 3:
4. __Polaristion modulation__
5. __Pulse-code modulation__
6. __Quadratic Amplitude Modulation (QAM)__

## Reasons for modulation
1. __Size of the antenna__ - the size of the antenna to is inversely proportional to the frequency of the radiated signals and the antenna must be 1/10 of the wavelength of the signal.  If the frequency signals are more than 5KHz in that case it is quite impossible to set up an antenna of that size. So, by using the modulation technique the size of the antenna is reduced.
2. __Minimize interference__ - if the frequency of the different senders and receivers are relatively far away from each other then they can send and receive messages simultaneously without any interference.
3. __Allow multiplexing of signals__
4. __Wireless Communication__ - modulation provides a wireless connection to transmit the signals to a longer distance
